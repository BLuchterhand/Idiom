# Source Gap Backlog

This backlog comes from antagonist red-team review. It is a live evidence-risk
ledger, not proof that every item still blocks beta release.

After the critique-remediation pass, the public promise is narrower: the book
teaches how to turn AI output into trusted work through an inspectable workflow.
It does not claim to prove that AI systems become empirically reliable across
all domains. Use this backlog to decide which claims may remain in the
manuscript, which must stay caveated, and which require more source work before
public launch or a formal publisher pitch.

## Highest Priority

### Scientific Grounding Of LLM Probabilism

Needed to support: a concise, accurate explanation of why LLM fluency is not
the same as grounded truth or authority.

Look for:

- Accessible but rigorous explanations of next-token prediction and sampling.
- Calibration and uncertainty research for LLMs.
- Mechanistic or empirical work on hallucination, confabulation, and
  faithfulness.
- Sources that avoid the misleading extremes of "just autocomplete" and
  "human-like understanding."
- Work on learned internal representations, in-context learning, emergent
  abilities, and world-model-like behavior that prevents the book from
  underclaiming LLM capability.

### Capability Increases Delegation And Control Requirements

Needed to support: "Capability improvements move the trust boundary; they do
not eliminate it."

Look for:

- Aviation automation and automation complacency.
- Clinical decision support and physician overreliance.
- Financial controls after increased automation.
- Safety-critical systems where better automation increased monitoring,
  certification, or procedural controls.
- Regulated decision systems where delegated authority required stronger audit
  and accountability.

### Verification Cost And Review Bottlenecks

Needed to support: accepted verified output as the proposed operating metric
for consequential workflows with verification bottlenecks.

Look for:

- Rework-cost studies.
- Review queue and inspection bottleneck studies.
- Customer support QA cost and quality evidence.
- Claims-processing audit/review studies.
- Legal/medical review workload evidence.
- Software review bottleneck evidence beyond AI coding studies.

### Customer Support And Operational Decision Workflows

Needed to support: the running case and the book's primary illustration domain.

Look for:

- Support quality-assurance programs and escalation workflows.
- Service-credit or policy-exception handling.
- Claims review, exception approval, or compliance-review workflows.
- Queue data, reviewer capacity, and review-cost evidence.
- Audit fields and source-of-truth practices in operational decision systems.

### Human Review Failure In Modern GenAI Contexts

Needed to support: "human review is not a valid control without time,
expertise, independence, incentives, and authority."

Look for:

- LLM automation bias studies.
- Hallucination acceptance studies.
- Legal AI citation hallucination incidents and analyses.
- Medical AI review and automation bias research.
- Code review under overload or reviewer-overload research.
- Studies where AI assistance changed reviewer confidence or error detection.

### Authority-Boundary Fixes That Fail Alone

Needed to support: Chapter 3's claim that prompts, RAG, human-in-the-loop
labels, policies, and eval dashboards are useful artifact fixes but incomplete
decision controls.

Look for:

- RAG failures caused by stale, irrelevant, or weak source authority.
- Eval dashboards that failed to predict operational failures.
- Human-in-the-loop labels without adequate reviewer evidence or rejection authority.
- Policies that did not change permissions, acceptance, or audit behavior.
- Cases where authority boundaries remained weak despite technical mitigation.

## Organizational Incentives And Failure Modes

Needed to address: artifacts do not enforce themselves.

Look for:

- Audit theater and compliance theater.
- Metric gaming.
- Incentive misalignment.
- Change-management failure.
- Shadow IT / shadow AI adoption.
- Governance arriving after tool adoption.
- Budget ownership for controls versus benefits.
- Named owners without real authority.
- Who receives productivity benefit versus who pays review cost.
- Rejection authority and reviewer independence.
- Reviewer SLAs and queue-management practices.

## Control Degradation Under Pressure

Needed to support: the human-process sidecar's claim that controls are useful
but not magic.

Look for:

- Normalization of deviance.
- Review without enough time or evidence under workload.
- Audit or compliance theater.
- Exception handling becoming the norm.
- Controls that degrade when incentives reward speed over correctness.

## Source-Quality Guardrails

- Treat consulting surveys as adoption-color, not proof.
- Treat vendor system cards as primary vendor claims, not neutral evaluation.
- Treat narrow developer productivity studies as domain-specific snapshots.
- Prefer peer-reviewed, government, standards, incident, court, regulator, or
  primary organizational evidence where possible.
- Reverify fast-moving reports before formal citation.
