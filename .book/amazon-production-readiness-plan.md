## Amazon Description

AI output becomes risky when a workflow treats it as accepted work before
evidence, authority, and review have caught up.

**Trust the Process, Not the Prompt** is a practical field guide for leaders and
senior practitioners moving generative AI from experiments into real
organizational work. It starts from a simple operating rule: AI outputs are
proposals. A workflow accepts them for use only after required evidence is
checked and a named owner accepts the decision or authorizes the permitted
effect.

The book is not a prompt-engineering manual, a tool catalog, or a warning that
AI is too unreliable to use. It explains how to use probabilistic systems well:
let them draft, synthesize, reason, translate, explore, and prepare candidate
work, then surround that work with explicit evidence, permissions,
verification, review capacity, accountable acceptance, and learning loops.

The central problem is not that people forget AI can be wrong. Serious teams
already know they need to check the output. The harder problem is that review
capacity is scarce, sources go stale, accountability lacks real rejection
authority, and
polished recommendations can spread through tickets, Slack, documents, and
customer workflows before anyone has decided whether they are current,
permitted, verified, or accepted.

Through a running support-credit workflow--a composite support exception
involving a possible service-credit recommendation--and examples from software,
operations, finance, medicine, aviation, law, and governance, the book shows how
to design one AI workflow your organization can trust, measure, and scale.

Readers will learn how to:

- distinguish useful AI proposals from accepted work;
- decide which tasks fit AI and which should remain human or escalated;
- map claims to authority-bearing sources and freshness requirements;
- define what AI may access, communicate, mutate, trigger, retry, or escalate;
- measure accepted verified output instead of generated volume;
- make human review meaningful by giving reviewers evidence, time,
  independence, expertise, and authority to say no;
- operate AI under pressure when shadow tools, review queues, incidents, and
  executive deadlines appear.

Better models may reduce many visible AI failures. That is progress, not a
rebuttal. As capability rises, delegation expands. The trust boundary moves,
but it does not disappear.

This book gives teams a concrete way to build that boundary.

## KDP Select Recommendation

Recommendation: **do not enroll in KDP Select for the first 90 days unless the
launch is Amazon-only and review acquisition is the only near-term goal.**

Reasoning:

- KDP Select gives Kindle Unlimited inclusion and Kindle promotional tools, but
  it makes the Kindle ebook exclusive to Amazon during the enrollment period.
- This book's best strategic use is as a durable thesis and credibility asset.
  That argues for keeping the ebook available for direct distribution, review
  copies, bundles with downloadable artifacts, and future non-Amazon channels.
- If the launch later needs an Amazon-only push, use one 90-day Select term
  after the landing page, reviews, and sample chapter are working.

Decision rule: choose KDP Select only if the first launch campaign depends on
Kindle Unlimited discovery or a Kindle Countdown Deal. Otherwise, publish wide
or keep direct distribution optional.

This is the launch review-copy plan, separate from the 8-12 person controlled
beta-reader plan in `production-readiness-plan.md`.

### Reader List

Build a list of 40 candidates so 25-30 can realistically accept.

- 8 engineering leaders or staff/principal engineers.
- 6 product, support, operations, or customer-success leaders.
- 6 governance, security, risk, compliance, legal, or audit readers.
- 6 AI practitioners building internal agents, copilots, or workflow tools.
- 4 skeptical executives/operators who care about cost, risk, or throughput.
- 3-5 non-specialist business readers for clarity.

### Package

Send a clean PDF or EPUB plus a one-page reviewer note. Include:

- the reader promise;
- the requested review deadline;
- a short disclosure that the support-credit workflow is a composite unless final sourcing
  changes that status;
- five feedback prompts;
- a polite request for an honest Amazon review after launch if the book was
  useful.

### Feedback Prompts

1. Where did the book earn trust, and where did it overclaim?
2. Which artifact would you actually use on Monday?
3. Where did the prose become abstract, slow, or repetitive?
4. Which claim needs stronger evidence before publication?
5. What would stop you from recommending this to a leader or senior
   practitioner?

### Timeline

- T minus 6 weeks: send beta/review copies.
- T minus 4 weeks: collect feedback and triage factual, structural, evidence,
  reader-handle, style, and preference comments.
- T minus 2 weeks: send final ARC and launch date to readers willing to review.
- Launch week: remind readers once with the Amazon link.
- T plus 10 days: one final reminder to readers who explicitly agreed to review.

## Downloadable Artifact List

The beta/proof package includes these public Markdown/CSV/XLSX files.
PDF/DOCX versions remain optional future production formats for the landing page.
The landing page should not require an email address for the basic versions;
use optional email capture only for expanded templates.

1. **Minimum Viable Decision Acceptance Contract**
   - One decision, one source of truth, one action limit before acceptance, one
     checker, one stop condition, one owner.
2. **Workflow Readiness Preflight**
   - Task tier, what the output may do before acceptance, evidence source, data
     boundary, reviewer capacity, stop condition.
3. **Claim-To-Evidence Matrix**
   - Claim, authority-bearing source, freshness requirement, evidence status,
     reviewer decision.
4. **Source Freshness Map**
   - Current source, stale/conflicting source, owner, refresh cadence,
     unresolved conflict path.
5. **Effect-Boundary Specification**
   - Access, communication, mutation, tool use, retries, escalation,
     post-action verification.
6. **Review-Capacity Calculator**
   - Proposal volume, reviewer-hours, average review time, queue threshold,
     throttle/stop rule.
7. **Reviewer Packet Template**
   - Claims, source links, stale-source warnings, missing evidence,
     accept/reject/escalate controls, rejection authority.
8. **Shadow-AI Bypass Tracking In The Control-Validation Loop**
   - Bypass location, reason, blocked need, lower-authority sanctioned path,
     control change. In the current beta/proof package, this is tracked inside
     the control-validation loop rather than as a standalone file.
9. **Eval, Incident, and Governance Loop**
   - Sampling, rejected-decision review, stale-source drills, reviewer
     calibration, escaped-error review, control retirement.
10. **First Workflow Design Canvas**
    - One-page synthesis for the 30-day pilot in Chapter 12.

## Low-Friction Essay Series

Publish 10 essays over 10-12 weeks. Each should stand alone at 900-1,500 words
and end with one concrete question or artifact field, not a sales pitch.

1. **AI Outputs Are Proposals**
   - Core distinction between generated work and accepted work.
2. **The Problem Is Not That People Forget To Check AI**
   - Why review fails in practice: time, evidence, incentives, source conflict.
3. **Capability Moves The Trust Boundary**
   - Why better models increase delegation and therefore raise the value of
     workflow controls.
4. **Human In The Loop Is Not A Control**
   - Conditions for useful human review.
5. **Context Is Not Authority**
   - Source hierarchy, freshness, and claim-level evidence.
6. **Measure Accepted Verified Output**
   - Why generated volume is the wrong adoption metric.
7. **When Artifact Fixes Do Not Control The Decision**
   - Prompts, RAG, evals, dashboards, and policies as useful but incomplete.
8. **What AI May Affect**
   - Access, communication, mutation, retries, escalation, and stop conditions.
9. **Shadow AI Is A Pressure Signal**
   - Treat bypass as evidence of unmet workflow need.
10. **Design One Trustworthy Workflow**
    - The 30-day pilot and minimum viable decision acceptance contract.

Publication order: start with essays 1, 2, 5, and 6 before launch because they
best communicate the book's wedge. Hold essays 8-10 for launch week and the
month after.
