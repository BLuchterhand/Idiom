# Positioning Memo

Purpose: make the book's differentiation explicit against adjacent books,
frameworks, and conversations. This is not a complete market scan; refresh it
before writing a formal proposal.

## Differentiated Claim

This book is not primarily about prompt craft, responsible-AI principles, or
"remember to verify outputs." Its central claim is narrower and more operational:

> AI-generated work becomes dangerous when it enters authority faster than the
> organization can verify, fund, stop, and learn from it.

The book's distinct contribution is a practical authority-boundary model for
AI-assisted operational decisions: how AI output is promoted from proposal to
authority through an enforceable decision acceptance contract, reviewer-capacity
math, and control-retirement tests.

## Title Positioning

Public title: **Trust the Process, Not the Prompt**

Subtitle: **How to Turn AI Output Into Trusted Work**

Brand doctrine:

> AI output starts as a proposal. A workflow accepts it for use only after
> required evidence is checked and a named owner accepts the decision or
> authorizes the permitted effect.

This should be the book's standing answer to adjacent AI adoption books. The
book is not selling prompt tricks, model enthusiasm, or inert governance
paperwork. It argues that AI can be genuinely useful precisely because
organizations do not have to treat model output as self-authorizing. They can
let AI propose, synthesize, draft, and reason while reserving authority for an
inspectable process. The subtitle is intentionally narrower than a broad
reliability promise: the book teaches a proposal-to-authority workflow, not a
guarantee of model reliability.

Sharper wedge:

- Not "AI governance" in general.
- Not "verify outputs" as advice.
- Not "agentic operating model" as a slogan.
- The book is about preventing AI-generated work from entering authority faster
  than the organization can verify, fund, stop, and learn from it.

## Adjacent Books

### Co-Intelligence: Living And Working With AI

Author: Ethan Mollick. Publisher: Portfolio, 2024.

Adjacent because: it gives mainstream readers a practical and optimistic way to
work with generative AI.

Different because: this book is less about individual collaboration habits and
more about organizational authority boundaries, verification capacity,
incentives, and operational workflow design.

Source: Penguin Random House page for *Co-Intelligence*:
https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/741805/co-intelligence-by-ethan-mollick/

### Human-Centered AI

Author: Ben Shneiderman. Publisher: Oxford University Press, 2022.

Adjacent because: it emphasizes reliable, safe, and trustworthy human-centered
AI design.

Different because: this book uses human-centered principles inside a specific
enterprise adoption problem: preventing AI output from becoming operational
authority without evidence, acceptance, and control validation.

Source: University of Maryland Human-Centered AI Lab page:
https://hcil.umd.edu/human-centered-ai/

### Prediction Machines

Authors: Ajay Agrawal, Joshua Gans, and Avi Goldfarb. Publisher: Harvard
Business Review Press, 2018.

Adjacent because: it frames AI economically as reducing the cost of prediction
and changing the value of judgment.

Different because: this book focuses on the post-generation trust boundary:
even when AI makes prediction, synthesis, or drafting cheaper, the organization
still has to verify, accept, fund, and control the resulting work.

Source to verify before formal proposal: publisher or author page.

### Competing In The Age Of AI

Authors: Marco Iansiti and Karim Lakhani. Publisher: Harvard Business Review
Press, 2020.

Adjacent because: it explains AI-centered operating models and digital scale.

Different because: this book is about the trust substrate inside adoption: how
operational decisions move from AI proposal to accepted work through rules that
can change acceptance.

Source to verify before formal proposal: publisher or author page.

## Adjacent Frameworks

### NIST AI Risk Management Framework

Adjacent because: it provides a widely used risk-management language for
trustworthy AI.

Different because: this book translates risk concepts into a trade-book
workflow narrative: a running case, a minimum viable decision acceptance
contract, reviewer capacity, shadow AI, and control validation.

Source: NIST AI RMF:
https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework

### ISO/IEC 42001

Adjacent because: it defines an AI management-system standard.

Different because: this book is not a certification or management-system guide.
It is a practical adoption argument for leaders and senior practitioners trying
to make a specific workflow trustworthy.

Source: ISO AI management systems overview:
https://www.iso.org/artificial-intelligence/ai-management-systems

### OECD AI Principles

Adjacent because: they define high-level principles for trustworthy AI.

Different because: this book starts where high-level principles often stop:
what happens in a real operational decision when evidence is stale, reviewers
are overloaded, tools sprawl, and incentives reward speed.

Source: OECD AI Principles:
https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/sub-issues/ai-principles.html

## Adjacent Conversations

### Prompt Engineering

Adjacent because: prompt quality can improve outputs.

Different because: prompts are treated as artifact fixes. The book's core
question is whether the workflow controls the decision, not whether the
instruction sounds better.

### Responsible AI / AI Governance

Adjacent because: the book shares concerns about risk, accountability,
transparency, and oversight.

Different because: the book treats governance as operational only when it can
change permissions, evidence requirements, acceptance, audit, escalation, or stop
conditions.

### AI Productivity

Adjacent because: the book accepts that AI can make useful work faster.

Different because: the book measures accepted verified output, not generated
volume. It treats review capacity, rework cost, and escaped-error cost as part
of the productivity equation.

## Positioning Sentence

For leaders and senior practitioners moving AI from experiments into dependable
work, this book explains how to keep probabilistic AI useful without letting it
grant itself authority. It shows how to design operational workflows where AI
can propose, synthesize, and act within boundaries, while evidence, reviewers,
systems, incentives, and governance decide what becomes trusted work.

## Market-Scan Gaps

## 2026 Self-Publishing Refresh

This refresh is for Amazon positioning, not a full traditional-publishing
proposal. It should be revisited before any formal agent or publisher pitch and
before treating market positioning as final.

Current adjacent market signals:

- Enterprise AI writing has moved from general adoption and productivity toward
  agentic AI, workflow integration, governance, and operating-model change.
- Newer books and guides increasingly frame AI agents as enterprise
  transformation infrastructure. For example, Springer/O'Reilly's *Agentic AI
  in Enterprise* / *Harnessing Agentic AI for Business Transformation* positions
  agentic AI around strategic roadmaps, integration, scalability, efficiency,
  and security:
  https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/979-8-8688-1542-3
- Enterprise guidance from AWS and similar vendors increasingly emphasizes
  governing and architecting agentic AI at scale:
  https://docs.aws.amazon.com/prescriptive-guidance/latest/govern-architect-agentic-ai/introduction.html
- Analyst, vendor, and practitioner reports increasingly discuss production AI
  agents, governance, ROI pressure, workflow orchestration, security, and
  accountability. Treat these as market color, not proof.

Updated differentiation:

This book should not compete as "the enterprise AI agent strategy book." That
shelf is crowded and vendor-shaped. Its better Amazon position is the
operational trust field guide underneath agent and AI adoption:

> AI output becomes useful at work only when the workflow can decide what it may
> do before acceptance, which evidence supports it, who can accept it, what
> review capacity exists, and when the process must stop.

Compared with agentic-AI books, this book is narrower and more concrete:

- not agent architecture in general;
- not transformation strategy in general;
- not governance framework catalog;
- not AI productivity booster;
- specifically: proposal-to-authority workflow design, reviewer-capacity math,
  effect boundaries, and control validation.

Amazon positioning should therefore emphasize:

- practical field guide;
- one trustworthy workflow;
- proposal versus authority;
- accepted verified output;
- human review that actually has evidence, time, and authority;
- bounded action rather than unconstrained agents.

Keep the old adjacent-book list for durable book-category context, but use this
refresh for self-publishing metadata, description, and essays.
