# Manuscript Completion Plan

Purpose: define the final manuscript target and the remaining editorial work
needed for a finished self-published field guide.

## Final Product Decision

Final target: **38,000 to 44,000 words**, including front matter, chapters,
appendices, bibliography, and production-facing back matter.

Rationale:

- The current generated manuscript is about 43,100 words.
- The book's strongest commercial fit is a practical field guide, not a padded
  65,000-word trade book.
- The reader promise is operational: design one trustworthy AI workflow. That
  promise benefits from concise chapters, worked artifacts, and appendices more
  than broad essay expansion.
- Expansion should happen only when it adds a real case, an objection, a worked
  artifact, a source-backed claim, or a concrete operating example.

## Expansion Rules

Do expand when the new material does one of these jobs:

- makes the support-credit running case more concrete;
- gives the reader an artifact they can reuse;
- answers an objection a real operator or executive would raise;
- adds source-backed evidence to a claim that currently reads empirical;
- clarifies an analogy break between human controls and AI mechanics;
- makes a stop, throttle, block, escalation, or acceptance state visible.

Do not expand when the new material:

- restates proposal-versus-authority without a new pressure or control;
- adds another adjacent AI incident with the same lesson;
- turns the book into an AI governance survey;
- makes current model limitations carry the durable argument;
- pads chapters to meet the old 65,000-word target.

## Objections To Answer

| Objection | Best Location | Required Answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "This slows us down." | Chapters 8 and 12 | Controls cost time but can save rework, escalation, and unsupported-review debt; queue math makes the tradeoff visible. |
| "Better models will solve this." | Chapters 1 and 13 | Better capability moves the trust boundary; it does not create organizational accountability, source authority, or acceptance by itself. |
| "We already have governance." | Chapters 3 and 11 | Governance counts only when it changes acceptance, permissions, evidence requirements, escalation, logging, throttling, or retirement. |
| "Human review is enough." | Chapters 6, 8, and 9 | Review works only with evidence, time, independence, capacity, and rejection authority. |
| "We need AI agents, not paperwork." | Chapters 7, 10, and 12 | Agency increases the need for explicit effects, failure states, permissions, post-action checks, and stop conditions. |
