## Chapter Claim

Trustworthy AI adoption starts with one narrow workflow whose evidence,
permissions, review capacity, stop conditions, and learning loop are explicit.

## Reader Starting Point

The reader may want an organization-wide AI program, tool rollout, or governance
policy before proving one workflow.

## First Visible Moment

A Week 0 setup plus three-week pilot begins with one support exception
workflow. The team hits one source-owner delay, one reviewer-capacity limit, one
shadow-AI discovery, and one stop/go decision.

Scene beats:

1. Week 0 exposes source ownership and data-class boundaries before any model
   debate.
2. Week 1 runs reviewed proposals only and auto-rejects incomplete packets.
3. Week 2 uses the Chapter 8 queue math to throttle proposal volume.
4. A bypass check finds private AI drafts and replaces them with a sanctioned
   draft assistance path.
5. The final review accepts low-risk reviewed proposals, escalates contract
   conflicts, and rejects direct credit automation for now.

## Workplace Behavior Parallel

Operational improvement usually starts by stabilizing one workflow before
scaling the method.

## Human Fallibility Pattern

Ambitious rollouts hide unclear ownership, weak evidence, overloaded reviewers,
and missing stop conditions.

## Existing Workplace Contract

Pilots use intake, source-owner negotiation, capacity planning, security review,
stop/go criteria, escalation scripts, and executive metric resets.

## AI Analogue

The first trustworthy AI workflow should exercise the decision acceptance contract
before the organization expands delegation.

## Analogy Break

AI adoption can scale usage faster than operating capacity; the pilot must test
bypass, review debt, stale evidence, and control costs from the beginning.

| Field | Pilot Assumption |

Final decision under pressure:

| Item | Final State |
| --- | --- |
| Accepted | Low-risk credit recommendations with current policy, contract cap, billing ledger, and customer-specific outage evidence. |
| Blocked | Recommendations missing current policy, contract clause, or reviewer capacity. |
| Escalated | Contract-policy conflict and executive override requests. |
| Shadow path | Private AI draft path replaced by sanctioned low-friction draft assistance path. |
| Automation rejected | Direct credit application remains off until post-action verification, audit, and capacity data improve. |

## Main Sections

1. **Week 0 plus three operating weeks**: narrative before checklist.
2. **Choose the workflow**: narrow, valuable, visible consequence.
3. **Fill the decision acceptance contract**: one decision, source, effect,
   checker, stop condition, owner.
4. **Run the operating cadence**: weekly queue, incidents, office hours,
   feedback, executive review.
5. **Make the final decision**: automate now, keep reviewed proposal, reject or
   delay.

## Practical Takeaway

Start with one workflow. Make failure visible. Scale only after the workflow can
accept, reject, escalate, throttle, and learn.

## Possible Objections

- "This is too small": small enough to learn is different from trivial.
- "Executives want quarterly impact": accepted verified output and avoided
  rework are the first credible metrics.
- "Our sources are messy": the pilot should expose exactly which source
  problems block trust.

## Final Payoff

End with a concrete decision under pressure: the workflow clarifies authority,
protects the reviewer, closes or absorbs a shadow path, and deliberately rejects
at least one tempting automation step.

## Bridge To Closing

The closing should restate the operating assignment: AI proposes and explores;
humans own judgment and accountability; workflows supply evidence, constraints,
memory, checks, recovery, and learning.
